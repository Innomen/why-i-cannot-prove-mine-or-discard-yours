# Why I Cannot Prove Mine or Discard Yours: Identity, Other Minds, and Irreparable Suffering in Experiential Empiricism

Brandon M. Sergent · ORCID 0009-0001-4165-5463

## Abstract

Personal persistence and other minds share an epistemic structure: present experience establishes its occurrence, while assigning another occurrence to the same or a different subject requires an additional inference. Experiential Empiricism (EE) makes this symmetry explicit without erasing the concrete distinction between directly felt suffering and another person's suffering reports. The present experiential card can contain an entire autobiography without establishing an owner who persists through the represented history. Experiential totality must include any other experiencing that exists, but neither totality nor uncertainty about personal boundaries establishes one universal subject or a shared metaphysical container. Applied to uploading, the distinction separates comparative preservation of ordinary continuity from proof that a particular experiencer survives. Applied to ethics, it shows why successors, compensation, memory alteration, and prior or subsequent consent cannot cancel a suffering occurrence. Ulysses pacts expose the further error of granting an earlier identity model unconditional authority over a presently suffering subject. The paper distinguishes this argument against cancellation from the Hedonic Core Framework's additional prohibition on suffering spikes, and identifies the externalist catastrophe at its smallest scale: one concrete sufferer subordinated to an abstraction about who owns, benefits from, or will eventually redeem the suffering.

**Keywords:** Experiential Empiricism, personal identity, other minds, temporal experience, mind uploading, Ulysses contracts, suffering spikes, experiential totality, Reality Repair Theory, Hedonic Core Framework
